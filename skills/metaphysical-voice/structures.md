# Structures: Building the Whole Piece

Sentence-level devices (see `rhetorical-devices.md`) are necessary but not sufficient — a piece also needs an overall shape. This file covers the common architectures for full pieces in this register, plus the specific technique for building invented cosmology that reads as ancient/coherent rather than improvised.

---

## Common full-piece structures

### 1. The Numbered Declaration Sequence (manifesto-native)

A series of numbered statements, each self-contained, building toward either a culminating final point or a circular return to the opening claim. This is the dominant structure for manifesto-register pieces (Futurist/Surrealist/Dada manifesto tradition; also usable for invented-doctrine fragments).

**Build pattern:**
- Open with the shortest, most declarative point — the thesis in miniature.
- Middle points can vary in length and may use any device from `rhetorical-devices.md`, but each should advance the argument or escalate the stakes, not just restate the opening in new words.
- The final point should either (a) be the longest and most intense — the culmination, or (b) circle back to echo the first point's exact phrasing with a twist, producing closure.
- Numbering itself is rhetorical, not just organizational — it performs system and confidence even when the underlying logic is loose. Don't apologize for or explain the numbering.

### 2. The Fragment-Collection (aphoristic/gnostic-native)

A sequence of self-contained short units (numbered or separated by white space/symbol), not narratively connected, each a complete thought. This is the structure of Nietzsche's aphoristic books, Heraclitus's fragments, and most gnostic sayings-collections.

**Build pattern:**
- Vary length deliberately — a collection of uniformly-sized fragments reads as mechanical. Mix one-line aphorisms with longer 3-5 sentence fragments.
- Do NOT create artificial narrative connective tissue between fragments ("Following from this..."). The gap between fragments is part of the form — the reader's mind does the connecting work, which is what makes the form feel inexhaustible rather than fully explained.
- Consider an organizing principle invisible at the surface but present in your own planning (e.g., fragments move from outer/cosmic to inner/personal, or from question to assertion) — invisible structure still produces a sense of coherence even when not stated.

### 3. The Sustained Invocation (oracular/ritual-native)

One continuous address, building through escalating intensity to a single climactic moment, then either resolving (a final image of stillness/peace) or cutting off abruptly (ending on the climax itself, unresolved).

**Build pattern:**
- Establish the addressee/invoked entity early and concretely (even if abstract, give it one grounding image — principle 1 in the main file) before the intensity escalates, or the climax has nothing to climax *toward*.
- Use the structural devices (anaphora, litany, polysyndeton) to build through the middle — this is where sustained repetition belongs.
- The ending is the highest-leverage line in the whole piece. Two strong options: (a) the shortest sentence in the entire piece, landing after a long buildup (silence-after-noise effect), or (b) a single image that re-contains everything said before it in miniature (a final concrete picture that retroactively makes all the abstraction legible).

### 4. The Dialogic / Catechism Form

Question-and-answer structure, where the questions are often rhetorical or asked by an unspecified "you" and the answers are oracular/aphoristic. This borrows the form of religious catechism and Socratic dialogue.

**Build pattern:**
- Questions should escalate in difficulty/stakes across the sequence — don't open with the hardest question.
- Answers should rarely directly answer the literal question — the gap between what's asked and what's answered is itself meaningful (a technique borrowed from koans: the answer reframes rather than resolves).
- This form pairs unusually well with apophasis in the answers (define by negation rather than direct statement).

### 5. The Single Dense Paragraph

No internal structure markers at all — one unbroken paragraph that must do all its work through sentence-level variation alone (mixing short/long, varying devices) since it has no architecture to lean on beyond pure sentence rhythm.

**Build pattern:**
- This is the hardest form to sustain well because it has nowhere to hide a weak sentence — every sentence is structurally exposed.
- Plan an internal arc even without visible breaks: typically concrete image → abstraction earned from that image → a turn (paradox or reversal) → a final landing line that's the heaviest sentence in the paragraph.
- Keep it shorter than you think it should be. A single dense paragraph that runs past ~150-200 words usually needs an internal break (even just white space) or it exhausts the device-density the form requires.

## Building Invented Cosmology That Reads as Ancient

The core technique, stated in the main SKILL.md: **invent fewer named things and reuse them with apparent familiarity, rather than inventing many things and explaining each.**

### Why this works
Real ancient/sacred texts that survive into translation almost always come from traditions where the audience already had the context — the text itself never had to explain "what is the Logos" or "who is Behemoth" because everyone already knew. We experience that pre-existing familiarity as *weight* and *age*, even though it was originally just shared cultural context, not literary skill. You can manufacture the same sensation by writing as if the reader already shares the context, even when you are inventing the context in the same paragraph.

### The technique, concretely

1. **Invent 3-5 named elements maximum** for a short piece (a force, a place, a figure, a law, a number/cycle). More than that and you're explaining a world instead of gesturing at one.
2. **Introduce each one exactly once, without definition**, as if in passing: *"...as it was in the time before the Unbinding..."* — never *"...as it was in the time before the Unbinding, which was when..."* The moment you add the "which was when," you've broken the illusion of shared prior knowledge.
3. **Reuse the same invented terms later in the piece**, ideally in a slightly different context that adds a new facet without contradicting the first use. This reuse — not the initial invention — is what produces the feeling of a coherent total system, because the reader now has two data points that imply a third, unstated thing connecting them.
4. **Let at least one invented element remain permanently unexplained.** Real esoteric texts always have a residue of unresolved mystery — naming everything fully is the single fastest way to drain the form of its power. If everything is explained, there's nothing left for the reader to feel they're circling.
5. **Internal consistency matters even without explicit explanation.** Keep a private mental (or literal) note of what each invented term means so that reuse doesn't accidentally contradict itself — inconsistency, even unexplained, reads as sloppy rather than mysterious. Mystery and incoherence are not the same thing, and readers can feel the difference even when they can't articulate it.

### Worked example of the technique in action

First mention: *"They had not yet learned to fear the Counting, back when the river still answered to its first name."*

Second mention, later, different context: *"The Counting does not care what you have done; it only cares that you are still here to be counted."*

Notice: neither sentence defines "the Counting." But by the second use, the reader has inferred: it's some kind of judgment or reckoning, tied to presence/existence rather than morality, ongoing rather than a single event, and personified enough to "not care." That's a real piece of cosmology, built with zero direct explanation — purely through consistent, confident reuse.

## Choosing structure based on the four calibrations (from main SKILL.md)

- **Authority = collective will** → Numbered Declaration Sequence
- **Authority = concealed knowledge** → Fragment-Collection or Sustained Invocation with apophasis-heavy texture
- **Authority = outside-time transmission** → Sustained Invocation, often ending unresolved
- **Authority = solitary insight** → Fragment-Collection, aphoristic, more combative tone throughout
- **High degree of invented system** → any structure works, but Fragment-Collection and Numbered Declaration both benefit most from the cosmology-reuse technique above, since they have multiple discrete units across which to plant and repeat invented terms
- **Pure atmosphere, low system** → Single Dense Paragraph or Sustained Invocation, leaning hardest on sentence-level devices and sound (principle 4) rather than cosmology-building
